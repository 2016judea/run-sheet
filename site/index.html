Dock Doors

Pick a door.

Ten ways to run a dock, a warehouse or a fleet better. Learned the hard way at the biggest one. Written for everyone else.

Each door is one idea about running a dock, a warehouse or a fleet. One line you can carry, what it looks like on your floor, why it is true, what to measure on Monday with what you already own, and the rule with its reason attached. No tool. No data. No pitch.

On the floor
  • 1 The signal is already there.
    Before you buy a system, join two signals your building already gives off.
  • 2 A queue only needs help when it has no way out.
    Sending people to a full lane pays where the work has nowhere else to go. Where the work can route around, it measures as nothing.
  • 3 Full is three different things.
    A sensor reports blocked. The container might be half empty. The trailer is a third question.
  • 4 Reaction is late by construction.
    Stations do not fill up. They get slammed. Staff to what is arriving, and set relative targets you can always hit.
  • 5 Measure the constant before you build the model.
    Somewhere in your system a round number nobody measured is deciding what is real.
Between buildings
  • 7 Size it before you build it.
    Find the number first. Let the size of the answer decide whether there is a project.
  • 8 Fewer touches, not faster touches.
    Every handoff is a place to lose a package and a day. Keep supply and demand in the same ring.
How to work
  • 6 Legibility beats intelligence.
    The best algorithm you will ever ship is beaten by a plan people can actually follow.
  • 9 Test it like a drug.
    Randomize on and off by the hour. Measure the neighbors. Keep the nulls.
  • 10 Go stand there.
    The organization is a bigger variable than the algorithm. Every correction worth making was found by walking the dock.
