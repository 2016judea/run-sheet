Door 2 · On the floor

A queue only needs help when it has no way out.

Sending people to a full lane pays where the work has nowhere else to go. Where the work can route around, it measures as nothing.

What it looks like on your floor

Two lanes, both full, both lit. On the first, every package for that destination has to come down this one lane. When it blocks, the sorter has nowhere to send that work. It circles, then it goes to the problem pile, and most of it misses its truck. On the second lane, the same destination is spread across six lanes. When one blocks, the sorter uses the other five. The light is on. Nothing is actually wrong.

Why it is true

A full buffer only hurts when it is the only buffer. If a destination maps to one lane, that lane is a dead end and a person is the only way out. If the destination is mirrored across many lanes, congestion routes around itself and your intervention is solving a problem the machine already solved. The same algorithm, the same signal and the same experiment can be decisive on one sorter and inert on the next. The difference is not the people or the software. It is how many ways out the queue has.

try it One destination, one lane or six. Block a lane.

Monday

  1. For every lane, count how many other lanes can take its work. One means a dead end. Five means a mirror.
  2. Rank your dead ends by volume. That is the list where staffing moves pay.
  3. Stop sending people to mirrored lanes on a light alone. Send them when the whole mirror set is full.

The rule, and why

Spend labor on dead ends. Let mirrors absorb themselves.

The cost of a move is a person walking across the building and leaving the work they had. Pay it only where it buys something.

The trap

Mirrored lanes still fail, but they fail together and later. When the whole set fills, the recirculation is worse because the machine kept everything moving right up to the edge. Watch the set, not the lane.
