Door 04 · On the floor · sheet 5

Reaction is late by construction.

Lanes don't fill up. They get slammed. Staff to what's inbound, and set relative targets your floor can actually hit.

On your dock

One body on a lane, handling a piece a minute, nothing recircing. Then the inbound triples. Half the freight starts circling. By the time two more people get there the lane is a wall and nine in ten pieces are missing the divert. Pull the data and the lane looked fine five minutes before. The lanes that end up with the most help had the least work right before it happened. It is not slow accumulation you can watch coming. It is a sudden arrival rate against a lane that looked healthy.

Why it is true

Any rule that fires on a full light is already late, because full is the end state. The information that would have helped was upstream: what got labeled in the last fifteen minutes and where it is bound. So compute headcount with units on it. Work on the lane divided by a measured rate, plus what is inbound in the next quarter hour, plus the quarter after that, weighting the present most because it is the only part you are sure of. Then set a relative target, not an absolute one. Do not try to staff every zone to its number, which the floor can rarely do. Make sure no lower-priority zone has more bodies than a higher-priority one. A target you can always reach beats an optimum you never can.

try it How many bodies does this zone need for the next half hour?

The present counts double, the near future more than the far. The weights are the shape of the idea, not a setting to copy. Measure your own rate.

Monday

  1. Measure the real rate per role off your own floor. Do not use the engineered standard. It varies by building and shift.
  2. Pull inbound by lane for the next 15 and 30 minutes from your label or induct data. If you have no forecast, what has been labeled and not yet sorted is one.
  3. Rank zones by heads needed against heads present. Move one body from the lowest-ranked zone with the most people. Repeat.

The rule, and why

Weight the present most, forecast the near future, and balance relatively.

The present is certain, the forecast is not, and an absolute plan the floor cannot execute trains people to ignore the plan.

The trap

Labor is one pool. Every body you move toward one crew is a body you moved away from another, and the far side of a move is where the next miss comes from. Measure the crew you pulled from as carefully as the one you fed.
