Door 3 · On the floor

Full is three different things.

A sensor reports blocked. The container might be half empty. The trailer is a third question.

What it looks like on your floor

A big box wedges at the top of a chute and trips the beam. The light goes on. The container under it is half empty. On the next lane a container really is full to the brim and the light is off because the beam sits above the pile. Down the row, a worker sees a light, closes the container early and wheels it to the door. The trailer leaves with air in it.

Why it is true

There are three fullnesses and they pull against each other. Sortation full: will the machine keep sending work here. Container full: is there room left in the box. Trailer full: did we ship cardboard or air. One sensor measures the first and gets used for all three. A light that means "stop sending" on one lane means "come close me" on another, and the same count of packages trips the beam at very different levels on different equipment. Treat the three as separate numbers and decide which one each action serves. Sometimes the right move on a lit lane is to shift packages, not to close.

Monday

  1. For each lane, record how many packages were on it each time the light came on. The spread tells you how much the light is worth there.
  2. Compare the count at the light with the count at close. The gap is your early-close loss.
  3. Put trailer fill next to lane lights on the same screen. When one goes up and the other goes down, you have found the trade.

The rule, and why

Name which fullness you mean before you act on a light.

Closing on sortation-full to protect the sorter is a transportation cost paid later, on a truck nobody on the dock will see.

The trap

The count that trips the beam depends on who is standing there. A person shifting packages lets more pile up before the beam blocks. Your threshold moves with your staffing, which is the thing you were trying to decide.
