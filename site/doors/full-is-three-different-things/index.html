Door 03 · On the floor · sheet 4

Full is three different things.

A photo-eye says blocked. The gaylord might be half empty. The trailer cube is a third question.

On your dock

A big box wedges at the top of the chute and trips the beam. The light goes on. The gaylord under it is half empty. On the next lane a gaylord really is full to the brim and the light is off because the beam sits above the pile. Down the row, the stager sees a light, closes the gaylord early and wheels it to the door. The trailer leaves with air in it.

Why it is true

There are three fullnesses and they pull against each other. Sortation full: will the sorter keep sending freight here. Container full: is there room left in the box. Trailer full: did you ship cardboard or air. One photo-eye measures the first and gets used for all three. A light that means "stop sending" on a palletize lane means "come close me" on a direct-drop lane, and the count of pieces that trips the beam differs wildly between a shoe sorter, a crossbelt and a direct-drop chute. Treat the three as separate numbers and decide which one each action serves. Sometimes the right move on a lit lane is to shift pieces, not to close.

Monday

  1. For each lane, log how many pieces were on it each time the light came on. The spread tells you how much the light is worth there.
  2. Compare the count at the light with the count at close. The gap is your early-close loss, and it shows up as air in the trailer.
  3. Put trailer cube next to lane lights on the same board. When one goes up and the other goes down, you have found the trade.

The rule, and why

Name which fullness you mean before you act on a light.

Closing on sortation-full to protect the sorter is a linehaul cost paid later, on a truck nobody on the dock will see.

The trap

The count that trips the beam depends on who is standing there. A body shifting pieces lets more pile up before the beam blocks. Your threshold moves with your staffing, which is the thing you were trying to decide.
