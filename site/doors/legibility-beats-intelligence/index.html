Door 6 · How to work

Legibility beats intelligence.

The best algorithm you will ever ship is beaten by a plan people can actually follow.

What it looks like on your floor

A system sends a worker a message: go to station A01-10108. They look at the message, look at the floor and keep working. The floor is painted with lane numbers. The message used the controller's address. Over one weekend the names in the messages were changed to match the paint on the floor. The share of messages people followed nearly doubled the next day. Three algorithms, a forecast integration and a year of tuning had not moved that number as much.

Why it is true

A recommendation nobody acts on has an effect of exactly zero, no matter how right it was. Adherence multiplies everything else. The person reading the message is mid-shift, mid-task, with a scanner in one hand. They will follow an instruction that costs them nothing to decode and ignore one that costs them a minute. Name things the way the floor names them. Say where to go in the words painted on the wall. Then measure adherence before you measure accuracy, because accuracy you cannot cash is not accuracy.

Monday

  1. Pull ten messages your systems sent to the floor this week. Walk to each place named. Could you find it from the message alone?
  2. Measure adherence: messages followed over messages sent, by shift and by role.
  3. Rename before you retune. If adherence is under half, the algorithm is not your problem yet.

The rule, and why

Every instruction to a person uses the floor's own names and fits on one screen.

The reader is attention-poor and the system is not. Spend the system's effort translating, never the reader's.

The trap

A jump in adherence over one day is one day. Keep the caveat attached. A rename that doubled adherence may also have arrived with a leadership visit, a slower shift and a new manager. Measure it again next week.
