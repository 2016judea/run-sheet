Door 08 · How to work · sheet 9

Legibility beats intelligence.

The best algorithm you will ever ship loses to a plan your floor can actually follow.

On your dock

A system sends a body a message: go to station A01-10108. They look at the gun, look at the floor and keep working. The floor is painted with door numbers. The message used the controller's address. Over one weekend the names in the messages were changed to match the paint. The share of messages people followed nearly doubled the next day. Three algorithms, a forecast integration and a year of tuning had not moved that number as much.

Why it is true

A recommendation nobody acts on has an effect of exactly zero, no matter how right it was. Adherence multiplies everything else. The person reading the gun is mid-shift, mid-task, with a case in the other hand. They will follow an instruction that costs nothing to decode and ignore one that costs a minute. Name things the way the floor names them. Say where to go in the words painted on the wall. Then measure adherence before you measure accuracy, because accuracy you cannot cash is not accuracy.

Monday

  1. Pull ten messages your systems sent to the floor this week. Walk to each place named. Could you find it from the message alone?
  2. Measure adherence: messages followed over messages sent, by shift and by crew.
  3. Rename before you retune. If adherence is under half, the algorithm is not your problem yet.

The rule, and why

Every instruction to a person uses the floor's own names and fits on one screen.

The reader has a case in one hand and the system does not. Spend the system's effort translating, never the reader's.

The trap

A jump in adherence over one day is one day. Keep the caveat attached. A rename that doubled adherence may also have arrived with a leadership visit, a slower shift and a new manager. Measure it again next week.
