Door 07 · Between buildings · sheet 8

Fewer touches, not faster touches.

Every handoff is a place to roll the freight and lose a day. Keep supply and demand in the same ring.

On your dock

A customer three towns over orders a thing you have in stock. It ships from a DC four states away because that is where the system found it, rides to a hub, gets sorted, rides to another hub, gets sorted again, and makes the promise by the skin of its teeth with five sets of hands on it. The same thing stocked nearby would have been one truck and two touches. Nobody made a bad call. The network just fills from wherever it still has capacity.

Why it is true

A national network fills orders from the nearest building until it cannot, and then from wherever. Each extra hub is a sort, a dwell, a trailer and a chance to miss. Carving the network into regions that each hold the selection their customers actually buy, and letting each region serve itself first, cuts touches and miles together, because they are the same thing seen from two sides. It also fills trucks: when most of a region's volume comes from a few buildings, the lanes from those buildings to the delivery stations run thick and direct. Thick lanes let you put the cutoff on the pallet instead of on the lane, which lets cutoffs move later. Speed and cost stop being a trade and start moving together.

Monday

  1. Count touches per piece from pick to door. Draw the distribution. The long tail is your cost.
  2. Measure the share of orders filled from inside the customer's region. Every point you add is miles and hands removed.
  3. Find the lanes that would run full if demand were served locally. Those are the lanes to make direct.

The rule, and why

Place inventory where it will be bought, and let the region serve itself first.

Touches are the one cost that compounds through every downstream stage, and placement is the one lever upstream of all of them.

The trap

Regionalizing is mostly a software setting, which means it is mostly an inventory problem. A region that does not hold the right selection serves itself badly and ships nationally anyway. The hard work is forecasting demand by place, not drawing the map.
