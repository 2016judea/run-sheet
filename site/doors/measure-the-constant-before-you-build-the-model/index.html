Door 05 · On the floor · sheet 6

Measure the constant before you build the model.

Somewhere in your WMS a round number nobody measured is deciding what is real.

On your dock

Someone pulls a piece off a lane without scanning it. The piece leaves the building. In the system it stays on the lane. Do that a few hundred times a day and your system believes a lane is full of work when it is empty, and it will confidently send someone there. The defense was a cutoff: anything sitting too long is assumed gone. The cutoff was a round number in a config file. Nobody had measured it. It was just round.

Why it is true

Ghosts are not a volume problem or an equipment problem. Two buildings with the same layout and the same conveyor differ by how often their people scan, and a small difference in scan compliance turns into a large error in what the system thinks is on the floor. Every model you build on top inherits the error. The fix is not a better model. Go measure the actual distribution of how long real work sits, find the tail, and set the cutoff there. You will give up a small slice of real work to evict every ghost, and that trade is obviously right once you can see both sides.

Monday

  1. Measure scan compliance per lane: pieces that left with a scan over pieces that left at all.
  2. Measure how long real work actually sits before it is handled, and find the 99th percentile. That is your cutoff, not a round number.
  3. Open every config file your decisions depend on and list the constants. Ask of each: who measured this, and when?

The rule, and why

No constant ships unmeasured. Every threshold carries the date and the data that set it.

A round number looks like a decision. It is usually a guess that survived because nothing downstream could audit it.

The trap

Compliance is a habit, not a property of the building, so it drifts. A cutoff measured once is a cutoff that will be wrong again. Re-measure on a schedule, or make the cutoff compute itself from the current distribution.
