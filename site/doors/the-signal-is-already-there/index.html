Door 1 · On the floor

The signal is already there.

Before you buy a system, join two signals your building already gives off.

What it looks like on your floor

Walk your outbound dock for one shift and watch the lead. They walk the lanes looking for one thing: a lane full of work with nobody on it. When they find one they radio someone over. They do this for eight hours. That is a person polling a signal. Now look above the lane. There is a light that turns on when the lane is full. There is a scanner in every associate's hand that says where they are and how fast they are working. The building already knows both halves of the lead's job. Nobody has joined them.

Why it is true

Most of the data a building needs to run itself already exists as exhaust. Sensors on the equipment, scan events on the handhelds, timestamps on every move, the schedule on the wall. Each lives in a different system owned by a different team, and each team reads its own. The gap is never the sensor. The gap is the join. A join costs a week. A new system costs a year, and it still needs the join.

Monday

  1. List every signal the floor emits: lights, photo-eyes, scan events, door sensors, trailer sensors, the shift plan. Write down who reads each one today.
  2. Find the human loop. Which signal does a person walk around checking? That is your first join.
  3. Join it to one other signal you already have and send one message. Measure what the message changed before you build anything else.

The rule, and why

Never buy a system to produce a signal you already emit.

A vendor sells the sensor and the dashboard. What you need is the join, and only you can do that, because only you own both sides.

The trap

The signal you can get is never quite the thing you want to know. A full-lane light says a beam is blocked, not that the lane is full. A scan says where someone was, not where they are. Treat every signal as a proxy and measure how far it sits from the truth. See door three and door five.
