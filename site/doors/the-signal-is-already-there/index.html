Door 01 · On the floor · sheet 2

The signal is already there.

Before you buy another system, join two signals your building already gives off.

On your dock

Walk your outbound wall for a shift and watch the lead. They walk the doors looking for one thing: a lane backed up with freight and nobody on it. When they find one they get on the headset and send a body over. They do this for ten hours. Now look above the lane. There is a light that comes on when the lane is full. There is an RF gun in every hand that says where that person is and what their rate is. The building already knows both halves of the lead's job. Nobody has joined them.

Why it is true

Most of what a building needs to run itself already exists as exhaust. Photo-eyes on the conveyor, scan events on the guns, timestamps on every move, the labor plan on the board. Each lives in a different system owned by a different vendor, and each reads its own. The gap is never the sensor. The gap is the join. A join costs a week. Another WMS module costs a year, and it still needs the join.

Monday

  1. List every signal the floor gives off: lane lights, photo-eyes, RF scans, door sensors, yard sensors, the labor plan. Write down who reads each one today.
  2. Find the human loop. Which signal does a person walk around checking? That is your first join.
  3. Join it to one other signal you already have and send one message to one gun. Measure what the message changed before you build anything else.

The rule, and why

Never buy a system to produce a signal you already give off.

The vendor sells the sensor and the screen. What you need is the join, and only you can do that, because only you own both sides.

The trap

The signal you can get is never quite the thing you want to know. A lane-full light says a beam is blocked, not that the lane is full. A scan says where someone was, not where they are. Treat every signal as a proxy and measure how far it sits from the truth. See door three and door five.
